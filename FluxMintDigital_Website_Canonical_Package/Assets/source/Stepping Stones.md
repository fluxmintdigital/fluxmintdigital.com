**Stepping Stones**  
  
There is a stream I keep imagining.  
  
Not a large one.  
  
Just wide enough that you cannot cross it in a single step.  
  
On the other side is something you want badly enough to reach.  
  
Maybe you can see the destination clearly.  
  
Maybe only part of it.  
  
The mistake would be staring at the far bank and asking:  
  
**How do I jump all the way over there?**  
  
You probably can’t.  
  
Most of the things I want to build work like that.  
  
⸻  
  
The garage I imagine does not exist yet.  
  
Not really.  
  
The building exists.  
  
The concrete floor exists.  
  
The walls are there.  
  
The dream is there.  
  
But the machines are not.  
  
The research infrastructure is incomplete.  
  
OmniShell is unfinished.  
  
Some of the capabilities I will need do not exist yet.  
  
Others exist but are far beyond what I can presently afford.  
  
If I measure my present position against the finished vision, the distance is enormous.  
  
That is a dangerous way to measure anything.  
  
⸻  
  
A stream is crossed differently.  
  
You look down.  
  
There is a stone within reach.  
  
Step onto it.  
  
From there, another becomes reachable.  
  
Then another.  
  
Some wobble.  
  
Some turn out to be too far apart.  
  
Sometimes you step backward.  
  
Sometimes you discover a better path halfway across.  
  
You rarely need to know every stone before taking the first one.  
  
You only need enough understanding to reach the next stable place.  
  
⸻  
  
That has gradually become how I think about building my life.  
  
Books can become stepping stones.  
  
A tool can become one.  
  
A piece of software.  
  
A small service.  
  
A website.  
  
A successful experiment.  
  
A failed experiment that teaches something valuable.  
  
A relationship.  
  
A skill.  
  
A machine.  
  
Revenue.  
  
Knowledge.  
  
Trust.  
  
Each one changes what becomes reachable afterward.  
  
⸻  
  
This is different from treating every project as merely a means to something larger.  
  
I don’t want to build disposable things.  
  
A good stepping stone still has to hold weight.  
  
The book should be worth reading even if the garage never exists.  
  
The tool should be useful even if OmniShell fails.  
  
The essay should matter even if nobody buys anything afterward.  
  
The individual thing deserves integrity.  
  
But integrity does not prevent connection.  
  
Something can be complete in itself and still become part of a larger crossing.  
  
⸻  
  
That distinction matters to me.  
  
I don’t want FluxMintDigital to become a pile of unrelated attempts to make money.  
  
I want it to behave more like an ecosystem.  
  
One thing feeds another.  
  
A book reveals a problem.  
  
The problem suggests a tool.  
  
The tool reveals a technical limitation.  
  
The limitation motivates infrastructure.  
  
The infrastructure enables a larger experiment.  
  
The experiment creates new knowledge.  
  
That knowledge becomes writing.  
  
The writing reaches someone who sees another possibility.  
  
And suddenly a path exists that none of the individual projects could have created alone.  
  
⸻  
  
This is why I sometimes resist the advice to “focus on one thing.”  
  
There is wisdom in focus.  
  
Scattered effort can destroy progress.  
  
But there is also a difference between scattering and sequencing.  
  
A forest is not unfocused because it contains more than one tree.  
  
What matters is whether the pieces participate in the same ecology.  
  
⸻  
  
The projects I am building are not identical.  
  
They shouldn’t be.  
  
A book is not software.  
  
Software is not research infrastructure.  
  
Research infrastructure is not a physical laboratory.  
  
A physical laboratory is not a story.  
  
But they can still belong to the same crossing.  
  
The question becomes:  
  
**What does this make possible that wasn’t possible before?**  
  
That question has become more useful to me than:  
  
**Does this look like the final destination?**  
  
Of course it doesn’t.  
  
It is a stone.  
  
⸻  
  
Some stones primarily create money.  
  
That is not something I am embarrassed by.  
  
Money is capability stored in another form.  
  
It can become equipment.  
  
Computing.  
  
Materials.  
  
Software.  
  
Time.  
  
Expertise.  
  
Freedom to pursue something that does not immediately pay for itself.  
  
I don’t want money to become the destination.  
  
But pretending resources don’t matter would be another way of refusing to look at reality.  
  
If the work is going to reach the physical world, eventually steel has to be purchased.  
  
Machines require electricity.  
  
Experiments consume materials.  
  
People need to eat.  
  
Resources are part of the architecture.  
  
⸻  
  
Other stones create knowledge.  
  
Those may be even more important.  
  
A project can fail financially and still answer a question that prevents years of future mistakes.  
  
A prototype can break and reveal the next constraint.  
  
A piece of writing can expose an assumption I did not realize I was carrying.  
  
A conversation can change the direction of an entire project.  
  
Progress is not always accumulation.  
  
Sometimes it is elimination.  
  
A stone can exist because you discovered where not to step.  
  
⸻  
  
This is part of why I want to document the path honestly.  
  
The polished version of a story makes every step look inevitable.  
  
Real crossings are messier.  
  
You test weight.  
  
You misjudge distance.  
  
You slip.  
  
You change direction.  
  
You discover that the stone you planned around isn’t stable.  
  
You notice another one you couldn’t see from shore.  
  
The path becomes visible while walking it.  
  
⸻  
  
That uncertainty does not bother me as much as it used to.  
  
I don’t need to know exactly what OmniShell will become twenty years from now.  
  
I need to know what architecture is worth building next.  
  
I don’t need to know every machine that will someday occupy the garage.  
  
I need to know which capability meaningfully expands the experiments I can run.  
  
I don’t need to predict the final shape of FluxMintDigital.  
  
I need to keep building things that deepen its ability to make useful work possible.  
  
The future can remain unresolved.  
  
Direction is enough.  
  
⸻  
  
There is also something gentler about stepping stones.  
  
They remove the tyranny of the giant leap.  
  
We love stories about giant leaps because the moment is dramatic.  
  
The company explodes.  
  
The invention works.  
  
The book changes everything.  
  
The person becomes successful.  
  
What disappears from those stories is the accumulation of small footholds underneath the moment.  
  
The years learning.  
  
The failed attempts.  
  
The boring infrastructure.  
  
The first customer.  
  
The first hundred dollars.  
  
The software nobody saw.  
  
The strange side project that later became essential.  
  
The friend who introduced someone.  
  
The thing built for one purpose that became useful for another.  
  
Most giant leaps are standing on something.  
  
⸻  
  
I think lives work the same way.  
  
We imagine transformation as crossing from one identity into another.  
  
But perhaps we become ourselves more gradually.  
  
One decision creates access to another.  
  
One act of courage changes the next decision.  
  
One failure removes an illusion.  
  
One relationship expands possibility.  
  
One boundary returns energy.  
  
One skill increases agency.  
  
Eventually we look backward and realize we have crossed a distance we could never have jumped.  
  
⸻  
  
This is how I think about the garage now.  
  
Not as a fantasy waiting for the day I suddenly possess enough money, technology, or expertise to build it.  
  
As the far bank of a crossing already underway.  
  
The books are part of it.  
  
The Observatory is part of it.  
  
The Architecture Wall is part of it.  
  
The Personal Architecture Map is part of it.  
  
OmniShell is part of it.  
  
The work I do for other people can be part of it.  
  
The things that succeed.  
  
The things that fail.  
  
The lessons inherited from people who came before me.  
  
All stones.  
  
⸻  
  
I am sure some of them will lead nowhere.  
  
That is fine.  
  
You do not cross a stream by demanding that every stone contain the entire route.  
  
You stand somewhere stable.  
  
Look again.  
  
Then decide what has become reachable.  
  
⸻  
  
Maybe that is how I intend to spend the time I have left.  
  
Not attempting one impossible jump toward some final version of my life.  
  
Building enough.  
  
Learning enough.  
  
Stewarding enough.  
  
To make the next thing possible.  
  
Then the next.  
  
Then the next.  
  
And someday, if I am fortunate, perhaps I will be standing in that old garage surrounded by machines, prototypes, books, tools, software, and strange experiments that once seemed impossibly far away.  
  
I suspect I will look back then and realize something.  
  
There was never one leap that got me there.  
  
There was only a path I could not see from the beginning.  
  
And a long series of stones I was willing to step onto.
